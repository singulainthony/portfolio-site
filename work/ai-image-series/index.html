Generative AI

Surreal stills

A series of quiet, uncanny images, from crystals holding galaxies to chairs above the clouds, made with my own multi-stage ComfyUI pipeline.

Generative AI is easy to do badly. Type a prompt, get something glossy, and post it. This series is the opposite: slow, quiet, slightly uncanny images where every frame looks photographed, not generated. They feature lone figures, impossible objects, and ordinary rooms where one thing is wrong.

The pipeline

Most of these images come from a ComfyUI workflow I built and keep refining. It has over 130 nodes and runs in two stages:

  • Composition pass: an SDXL checkpoint, sped up with SDXL-Lightning, sketches the layout, light, and mood quickly so I can iterate on ideas instead of waiting on renders.
  • Refinement pass: ControlNet locks that composition in place while a Z-Image Turbo model re-renders it at full fidelity, for realistic texture and light without losing the idea.
  • Finishing: a tiled upscale, sharpening, and film grain, so the results hold up big and read as photographs.
  • Variation: wildcard prompts generate controlled variations, so one idea becomes a series instead of a single lucky shot.

A few pieces (the hallway, the mask, and the dunes) were made on-device in Draw Things. The crystal islands came from an experiment where a local LLM running in Ollama writes and rewrites the prompts itself, and I direct it like a creative partner.

Why it matters for clients

The same workflow can be pointed at a brand. You get a consistent look across a whole campaign, imagery you own and can iterate on overnight, and none of the “obviously AI” sheen that makes people scroll past.
